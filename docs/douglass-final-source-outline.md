## Chapter IX

- **9.1**: March 1832 return; Thomas; hunger; Eliza/sister, Priscilla/aunt, Henny; four kitchen workers and spoiled stores.
- **9.2**: Thomas’s marriage-acquired ownership, inconsistent rule; August 1832 Methodist conversion increases cruelty; visiting preachers; Cookman sympathy and belief about Harrison emancipation.
- **9.3**: Wilson’s New Testament school broken up after three meetings by West, Fairbanks, others.
- **9.4–9.5**: Scripture used to justify Henny’s punishment; childhood burns, disabled hands; abandonment rather than care.
- **9.6**: Horse/Hamilton food strategy; nine months with Thomas; year hired to Covey, reputation for breaking youth, cheap labor; glad for sufficient food.

## Chapter X

- **10.1–10.5**: January 1, 1833; first field work; oxen/cart/gate accidents; whippings, brief meals, long work; Covey’s surveillance; Caroline and forced reproduction/twins counted as wealth; exhaustion erodes reading and hope.
- **10.6–10.9**: Sunday stupor, alternating hope and despair; Chesapeake white sails; apostrophe contrasts freedom/captivity; imagined northward route is not the successful escape route.
- **10.10–10.11**: Heat collapse fanning wheat; Smith/Hughes/Eli; attack; seven-mile journey to Thomas, refused protection; woods; Sandy’s hospitality and root belief; Sunday kindness; Monday stable resistance; Hughes helps, Bill refuses; two-hour struggle.
- **10.12–10.14**: Restored confidence and resolve; no further Covey whipping; remains enslaved four years; his inference that Covey feared losing his breaker reputation.
- **10.15–10.18**: Christmas 1833 ends Covey year; six-day holidays and coerced drunkenness as a safety valve; molasses anecdote; distorted freedom discourages rebellion.
- **10.19–10.23**: January 1, 1834 Freeland; frank, better meals/tools/time but still enslaver; Weeden/Hopkins religious cruelty; Henry and John Harris, Sandy, Handy Caldwell; footnote reiterates Sandy’s belief.
- **10.24–10.26**: Sabbath and winter-evening teaching; more than forty learners; unnamed free Black host protected; danger of 39 lashes; one learner freed through his agency; friendships, solidarity, desire to be own master.
- **10.27–10.29**: Hired again in 1835; escape planning and imagined dangers; Hamlet passage; Patrick Henry comparison with greater danger.
- **10.30–10.35**: Sandy withdraws; Douglass/Henry Harris/John Harris/Henry Bailey uncle/Charles Roberts married aunt; canoe Hamilton, Easter Saturday, bay head 70–80 miles; North Star; fishing disguise; forged Hamilton passes dated 1835; Baltimore cover only.
- **10.36–10.41**: Group pledge; Saturday foreboding, suspected betrayal; constables/arrest; Henry resists; Douglass burns pass, Henry eats pass with biscuit; Betsy Freeland abuse; own nothing; Easton jail, Joseph Graham; split rooms; informant not named by master.
- **10.42–10.45**: Traders inspect; companions removed, Douglass alone; separation most painful; Thomas retrieves him; Alabama intended but not carried out; returns to Hugh/Baltimore after three years and one month; feared community violence.
- **10.46–10.48**: Gardner shipyard on Fell’s Point; hurried brigs, contradictory orders; racial exclusion/free Black workers; four apprentice attackers; Sophia tends wounds; Hugh seeks justice; Watson will accept only white witnesses; none testify.
- **10.49–10.50**: Walter Price yard under Hugh; caulking mallet/irons; skilled wages $1.50/day, often $6–7/week; self contracts and collects; Hugh takes every cent; improved conditions increase freedom desire.

## Chapter XI

- **11.1–11.2**: Protecting escape means/helpers; condemns publicity giving enslavers information, upperground railroad distinction.
- **11.3–11.4**: 1838 wage robbery; hire-time requests, Thomas refusal/Hugh agreement; $3/week to Hugh plus board/clothes/tools; May–August; camp meeting late payment, revoked arrangement; defiance then outward contentment conceals plan.
- **11.5–11.6**: September 3, 1838 New York successful escape; means withheld; painful separation from friends; immediate joy gives way to isolation and kidnapping fear.
- **11.7–11.12**: David Ruggles boardinghouse Church/Lespenard Streets, shelter and advice New Bedford over Canada; Anna Murray already free arrives; James W. C. Pennington marries them September 15; certificate Frederick Johnson, witnesses Ruggles/Mrs. Michaels.
- **11.13–11.15**: Ruggles $5/letter to Shaw; John W. Richmond steamboat to Newport, stage to New Bedford; Ricketson/Taber friendly; Nathan/Mrs. Johnson shelter, fares/baggage; original Frederick Augustus Washington Bailey, Bailey, Stanley, Johnson, Douglass; Lady of the Lake suggestion, retains Frederick.
- **11.16–11.18**: Prosperous New Bedford contradicts wealth-requires-slavery assumption; dignified working homes; Black community solidarity and protection against informer.
- **11.19–11.20**: Third arrival day loading oil, first earnings own/Anna; discrimination prevents caulking; Johnson saw/wood-horse; wood/coal/chimney/cask work; footnote says caulking situation reportedly improving at time of writing.
- **11.21–11.22**: Liberator subscription four months after arrival; anti-slavery meetings; William C. Coffin encourages Nantucket speech August 11, 1841; initial hesitancy then confidence; public advocacy ends main Narrative.

## Appendix

- **12.1–12.5**: Clarifies Christianity of Christ vs American slaveholding religion; professed family, marriage, literacy, wages contradicted by slavery; pulpit/slave trade alliance; poem and biblical hypocrisy parallels; rituals versus justice/mercy/faith; complicit churches north and south.
- **12.6–12.8**: Copied parody attributed, not definitively authored, to a northern Methodist preacher; heavenly union refrain exposes piety beside cruelty.
- **12.9–12.12**: Hope book exposes slavery/hastens deliverance; reliance truth/love/justice; renewed pledge; Frederick Douglass signature, Lynn Massachusetts April 28, 1845; THE END.
